Where am I looking?

    How a star tracker thinks

    Six steps from a noisy picture to an orientation accurate to a few arcseconds, with an honest "I don't know" when it can't.

    1. 01

      Render

      Each catalog star in the 20° field becomes a small Gaussian blob on a 1024×1024 sensor, plus background and read noise. This is the simulated photograph.

    2. 02

      Detect

      Pixels 5σ above the background that are the brightest in their 3×3 neighbourhood are stars; a 5×5 brightness-weighted mean finds each centre to about 0.1 pixel, or 7 arcseconds.

    3. 03

      Vote

      The angle between two stars doesn't depend on where the camera points. For every pair of dots, the tracker looks up all catalog pairs with nearly the same angle (266,153 pairs, sorted, binary search) and votes for their stars. The right star collects a vote from almost every pair it is in; wrong ones only collect coincidences.

    4. 04

      Check

      Names that disagree with the others, from fake stars or unlucky votes, are dropped before anything is solved.

    5. 05

      Solve

      Wahba's problem by SVD gives the single rotation that best maps catalog directions onto camera directions: the attitude.

    6. 06

      Verify

      With that attitude, at least 4 dots must land within 25″ of catalog stars, or the answer is "no solution". It never guesses.
